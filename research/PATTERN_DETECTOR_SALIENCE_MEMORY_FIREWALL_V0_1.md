# D01 DL-028 — Detector-Selection Salience vs Structural-Memory Firewall V0.1

Updated: 2026-10-04 Asia/Taipei
Status: CLASS_A_RESEARCH_ONLY / OUTCOME_BLIND / SALIENCE_MEMORY_FALSIFICATION / FORMAL_CORE_LOCKED

## 1. Purpose

DL-027 froze parent-specific non-anchor horizontal pseudo-zones.

If a confirmed structural zone later outperforms those non-anchor controls, that result is still not sufficient to claim structural memory.

A confirmed zone is selected by a detector that preferentially identifies visually/statistically salient historical locations such as local extrema, repeated turning regions, wide excursions, high-volume reactions, round/tick-concentrated prices or long-lived anchors.

Therefore the next falsification question is:

> Does a confirmed structural zone contain representation beyond the salience created by the detector's own candidate-selection process?

DL-028 freezes a second control family to separate:
A. generic horizontal-level effects;
B. detector-selection / anchor-salience effects;
C. incremental confirmation / structural-memory effects.

No return outcome is inspected in this tranche.

## 2. Evidence context

Relevant external evidence is consistent with more than one mechanism:

- Lo, Mamaysky and Wang (2000) formalize chart-pattern recognition because visual pattern selection is subjective; automated detection is necessary before statistical comparison.
- Osler (2000) finds published support/resistance levels predict intraday FX trend interruptions better than arbitrary levels.
- Osler (2003) documents clustering of stop-loss and take-profit orders around round numbers and offers a microstructure explanation for reversals near and acceleration through technical levels.
- Tsinaslanidis and Zapranis (2012) identify horizontal support/resistance from historical local extrema; some interruption predictability exists, but excess-return superiority is not established.
- Henderson, Jacka, Liu and Maeda (2026) model support/resistance as a path-dependent state process; this is useful mechanism framing, not empirical proof of D01 alpha.

Together these sources justify testing structural levels, but they do not identify whether any observed edge comes from remembered structure, detector-selected salience, order clustering, or generic path dependence.

## 3. Three-layer mechanism ladder

### M0 — NON_ANCHOR_HORIZONTAL_CONTROL
Authority: DL-027.

Same symbol/date/width environment, but the boundary is not a detector-selected structural anchor known at the parent cutoff.

Purpose:
control generic horizontal-level crossing and opportunity effects.

### M1 — DETECTOR_SALIENCE_CONTROL
New in DL-028.

A candidate produced by the same detector family and candidate semantics as the true zone, already observable before the true-zone landmark, but still UNCONFIRMED_ACTIVE at that landmark.

Purpose:
control salience induced by candidate selection itself.

### M2 — CONFIRMED_STRUCTURAL_ZONE
The actual confirmed structural zone.

Purpose:
contains detector salience plus pre-landmark confirmation history.

Interpretation ladder after DL-026 opportunity controls and D16 inference:

- M2 ~= M1 > M0:
  detector-selection salience is sufficient; structural-memory interpretation weakens.
- M2 > M1 > M0:
  confirmation/history may add representation beyond detector salience, but causal memory is not yet proven.
- M2 > M1 ~= M0:
  strongest confirmation-specific representation candidate; still observational, not alpha proof.
- M1 > M2:
  detector salience may dominate while confirmation adds no benefit or creates over-conditioning; treat as falsification evidence.
- M2 ~= M1 ~= M0:
  generic level-crossing / opportunity explanation strengthened.

No branch of this ladder directly authorizes a Formal factor.

## 4. Confirmation landmark and risk-set semantics

For each true confirmed zone define:

- candidateAt: first time the true zone existed as a detector candidate;
- confirmationLandmark: the true zone confirmedAt timestamp;
- asOf: confirmationLandmark.

The salience-control risk set may include only detector candidates that, as of confirmationLandmark:

1. already existed causally;
2. were generated by the same detector version/family and semantic space;
3. belong to the same symbol for V0.1;
4. remain UNCONFIRMED_ACTIVE;
5. have complete candidate-state/session/continuity provenance;
6. are not the true candidate itself;
7. do not overlap the true zone boundary.

A control candidate that confirms later remains eligible if it was genuinely unconfirmed at the frozen landmark.

Future confirmation, future invalidation or future returns may never retroactively alter the frozen risk set.

## 5. Why UNCONFIRMED_ACTIVE candidates are the primary salience control

A random historical close does not reproduce detector selection.

An unconfirmed active candidate has already passed the same first-stage anchor/salience logic but has not yet accumulated the confirmation history of M2.

This makes M1 a stronger mechanism control for detector salience.

It is not a guaranteed null:
- some unconfirmed candidates may still be economically meaningful;
- non-confirmation may reflect insufficient exposure;
- candidate histories may differ in volatility, liquidity, distance path, age and crossing opportunity.

Those differences must be handled as pre-outcome covariates, not ignored.

## 6. Frozen pre-landmark salience / opportunity covariates

Store rather than score:

### Anchor salience
- anchorProminenceAtr;
- initialExcursionAtr;
- localExtremumRank or equivalent detector-native prominence;
- anchorVolumeRel;
- anchorRangeRel;
- gap/limit-constrained context where applicable.

### Price-grid / attention salience
- roundPriceDistanceTicks;
- tickSize / relativeTick;
- price level;
- round-number class if owner data provide it.

### Exposure / opportunity
- candidateAgeEligibleSessions;
- observableSessions;
- constrainedSessions;
- crossingOpportunitySessions or the canonical DL-026 opportunity receipt;
- distance-path summaries available causally by the landmark.

### Environment
- volatility / ATR;
- liquidity state;
- D02 acceptance/persistence context;
- market/sector regime;
- semantic-space and corporate-action continuity receipts.

No scalar SALIENCE_SCORE is created in V0.1.

## 7. No outcome-tuned matching

D01 freezes the eligible M1 risk-set manifest and covariate vocabulary.

D01 does not:
- select the closest control after returns are known;
- choose a caliper after outcomes;
- discard controls because they behave badly;
- tune a salience score;
- choose a matching estimator;
- optimize the number of controls.

D16 owns future weighting/matching/inference and must preregister it before outcome access.

All eligible M1 candidates remain in the frozen manifest.

## 8. Exposure and immortal-time firewall

Confirmed M2 zones necessarily survived long enough to confirm.

Therefore comparing M2 against arbitrary candidates without exposure control can create survivorship / immortal-time bias.

V0.1 requires:
- both M1 and M2 state measured at the same true-zone confirmation landmark;
- candidate histories truncated at that landmark;
- no future state used to define M1 eligibility;
- exposure/opportunity covariates retained;
- future outcomes start strictly after the immutable landmark when D16 eventually opens the join.

NOT_YET_CONFIRMED is a state at the landmark, not a statement that the candidate will never confirm.

## 9. Detector-version firewall

Controls must come from the same detector version/family as the true zone.

If the detector definition changes:
- do not mix candidate populations silently;
- create a new detector-version stratum;
- preserve old manifests and failed controls.

A result that exists only under one detector implementation is detector-specific evidence until independently replicated.

## 10. Structural identity / overlap firewall

A candidate that overlaps the true zone may be a duplicate representation of the same latent structure.

Such candidates are excluded from M1.

Known duplicate/relationEpisode identity should be excluded using only information available by the landmark.

Future-discovered structures may not retroactively clean the control set.

## 11. Relation to round-number/order-clustering mechanisms

Round-number proximity is not treated as structural memory.

If M2 advantage disappears after:
- M1 detector-salience controls;
- round/tick proximity;
- liquidity/volatility/opportunity controls;

then the structural-memory story is weakened.

If a robust M2 increment remains, D01 may call it:

STRUCTURAL_CONFIRMATION_SPECIFIC_REPRESENTATION_CANDIDATE.

It is still not:
- causal proof of remembered supply/demand;
- alpha proof;
- a trading rule.

## 12. Required manifest fields

Each salience-control manifest stores:

- trueParentId / relationEpisodeKey;
- trueCandidateId;
- symbol;
- semanticSpace;
- detectorVersion;
- confirmationLandmark;
- controlCandidateId;
- controlCandidateAt;
- controlStateAsOf;
- control boundary;
- salience covariate snapshot;
- opportunity/exposure receipt references;
- candidate-state/session/continuity provenance;
- exclusion reason where applicable;
- manifest hash/version.

The manifest itself is immutable after outcome opening.

## 13. Falsification outcomes

Future research should classify, not narratively rescue:

S0_GENERIC_LEVEL:
M2 does not beat M0.

S1_DETECTOR_SALIENCE:
M2 beats M0 but not M1.

S2_CONFIRMATION_SPECIFIC:
M2 beats both M0 and M1 after frozen opportunity/salience controls.

S3_UNSTABLE_OR_REVERSED:
M1 beats M2 or result changes materially across detector versions/regimes.

S4_NOT_EVALUABLE:
insufficient eligible M1 risk-set coverage or provenance.

Only S2 warrants continued structural-memory research.
None of S0-S4 alone authorizes Formal optimization.
